FIBRE FEATURES

interview series & social campaign

Fibre Features began as an investigation into material memory — the question of what a fabric retains after it has been stretched, saturated, abraded, and returned to rest. Over eighteen months, samples were accumulated from producers across four continents: raw wools pulled directly from clip, synthetics recovered from industrial overstock, reclaimed silks stripped of their original dyes and re-processed through mineral baths to restore a quality of surface that manufacture typically erases. Each material was subjected to a systematic sequence of tests before being considered for the collection — not to determine suitability in a conventional sense, but to map the outer boundaries of what each could be asked to do before it ceased to be itself.

The design process that followed was deliberately open-ended. No predetermined silhouettes were established at the outset; instead, each textile was allowed to propose its own structural possibilities. A loosely woven hemp found its natural form as a wide-cut coat with deeply dropped shoulders, the warp threads left deliberately proud at the hem to create an unfinished fringe that contracts and expands with humidity. A bonded double-cloth, stiff enough to hold a sculptural seam without interfacing, became the basis for a series of structured torso pieces whose geometry shifts depending on whether they are worn buttoned or open. The archive of samples functioned less as a resource than as a collaborator, each material contributing something the designer's hand alone could not have anticipated.
