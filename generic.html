TEMPLATE

Subheading

NAROD, which translates directly to "people," became a key rhetorical device of Soviet propaganda as early as the 1860s. This worked to impart a sense of shared heritage and emphasize Soviet collectivism, while simultaneously enforcing a process of modernization and severing ties to traditional ways of life for the diversity of minority populations it governed.

The collection seeks to reclaim that word, turning its meaning upside down; a celebration of the people not as a mass, but as a vibrant, textured, and irreducible community with its own voice and visual vocabulary.

"Narod" is both an homage and an evolution, a testament to the idea that tradition isn't static; rather, it lives on and grows along with us.
